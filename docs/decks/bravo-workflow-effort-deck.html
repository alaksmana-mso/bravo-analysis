01/Starting position

What most people believe about Bravo’s workflows is not what production shows

“One giant workflow handles all products” describes a shared spine that carries 5.5% of applications and exactly one product. What is actually expensive today is the older, one-workflow-per-product design — which is, almost exactly, the design usually proposed as the fix.

The giant workflows are the per-product ones

The shared spine is 43 top-level nodes across 33 files. NDF4W is 223 and NDF2W is 174. The older generation is the one-workflow-per-product design, and it is where the complexity built up: 156 different delegate beans across the older roots, against 69 for the five products on the newer one.

A gradual replacement has already started by itself

The shared spine has run DF4W in production for months, steadily, at 5.5%. Nobody planned it as a migration, but it proves the shape we want can carry real volume. The programme below is mostly about doing that on purpose.

03/Target versus today

Eight principles define best practice. Bravo meets one and a half.

PrincipleWhere Bravo is todaySeverity
A  The diagram is the truth: structure in the diagram, data in configStructure is encoded as is_active rows for steps that do nothing. 55 classes are gated this way. You cannot work out the real path from the diagramHigh
B  Each product owns a thin spine of its ownOne shared spine serves all five products, and the product is chosen by gateways and a selector variable. No product owns a readable spineHigh
C  Shared sub-workflows, with no product logic inside them19 of 68 shared steps branch on application.isXxx(), across 42 call sitesHigh
D  Split a sub-workflow only when the structure genuinely differsDone correctly once — …Underwriting_Regular. Everywhere else, differences are handled by switching steps off in config. The good example is the exceptionMedium

F  The engine should have only the rights it needs, and deploy per product/camunda is set to permitAll. /engine-rest gives full control of the engine to anyone holding the shared key — this is how the injected code got in. And there is one deployment unit for all productsHigh (security)

Bravo has the right building blocks, and one correctly-split sub-workflow. What it does wrong is express product differences in three places the diagram cannot show. So no product has a readable spine of its own that can be deployed independently — and the older monoliths carrying 94% of volume were never migrated at all.

04/What we would be building

A spine per product, shared sub-workflows, and differences out in the open

Keep — what the newer design got right

  • The top-level workflows contain no human tasks. Human work sits only in the sub-workflows. Production confirms it: the top-level workflows recorded no user tasks at all in 90 days
  • Each domain step exists once. Adding DF2W Sharia in 2026 touched the spine in one commit plus some migrations, instead of adding a fourth monolith
  • Escalation, cancellation and rejection are handled in one place, as boundary events on the spine, rather than redrawn for every product
  • The right kind of split already exists: Unified_Process_Workflow_Underwriting_Regular is a DF2W-family version called from the shared underwriting workflow. The team knows the pattern. It is just not applied consistently

Change — five rules, in order of payoff

  • 1 · One thin spine per product (about 40 nodes). It is the single source of truth for the order of stages and which sub-workflow each stage calls. The spine does the selecting, so the gateways go away
  • 2 · No silent skipping. A task that does not apply to a product should not be on that product’s path at all. At the very least, a missing config row must fail loudly
  • 3 · Take application.isXxx() out of the 19 shared steps. Product behaviour belongs in the spine or in a product-specific sub-workflow, never in a shared bean
  • 4 · Split a sub-workflow only when the structure differs — a different order of steps, different human roles, different partners. A different threshold does not count
  • 5 · Publish the real flow for each product, generated on every build and diffed in CI

Camunda 7 makes the middle path cheap

A call activity’s calledElement can be an expression. So a product spine can send most products to the shared survey sub-workflow and one product to survey_sharia, with no gateway and no flag. Five spines of about 40 nodes each cost far less than the 25 per-product sub-workflows a full split would need — and duplicating everything is exactly how NDF4W and NDF2W drifted apart in the first place.

05/How the numbers were built

Where these estimates come from — and how much to trust each phase

How the effort is counted

Engineer-days of a competent engineer who already knows ms-bpm, including that engineer’s own testing but not UAT run by business users. 21 working days to the engineer-month. Rupiah figures use the assumption applied throughout this pack: Rp30–50M fully loaded per engineer-month — substitute BFI’s own rate card. Ranges are low to high. They are not confidence intervals.

Unlike the end-of-support numbers, these did not come from a team memo. We derived them here by counting what is there — delegates, branch sites, gated classes, user tasks, production volumes — and the squads who would do the work should re-estimate them.

The one measurement that would firm up Phases 3 and 4

Of NDF2W’s 67 delegate beans, exactly one (checkUnderwritingNeededUnifiedActivity) is also used by the shared sub-workflows. So migrating is not a matter of pointing things somewhere else. It means working out, bean by bean, whether the shared sub-workflow already does that job.

Mapping the delegates — 5 to 8 days — would turn the widest range in this deck into a real number. It is the direct equivalent of the LORA coverage-gap inventory.

How confident we are, by phase

PhaseConfidenceWhy
P1 visibilityHighThe queries exist (§8.4) and so do the parse scripts. It is tooling work of a known shape
P2 prove on DF4WMedium-highYou can list the scope exactly: one spine, 3 gateways, 21 isDF4W() sites, and a known set of gated KYC and RAC steps
P3 NDF2WLow-mediumIt depends on how much of NDF2W’s behaviour the shared sub-workflows already cover, and nobody has measured that. The widest range here

What every number here leaves out

UAT run by the business, product-owner workshop time, rewriting domain logic (how survey and underwriting actually behave is out of scope), and any change to the LORA or Temporal track.

No workflow moves, no BPMN is restructured, and no product migrates. Phase 1 changes what can be seen, and what can be added. It makes each product’s real path visible and stops the number of hidden branches rising. Every structural change waits for Phase 2, behind a signed-off ADR.

11/If we do nothing

What leaving things as they are costs, measured rather than asserted

Every change can break everything

Commit messages on unified-main-workflow.bpmn name DF4W, DF2W, Sharia, RO, NDF4W and Company. Every one of those redeployed the spine that all five products run on. On the survey sub-workflow, DF4W work changed the orchestration NDF2W also uses. One deployment unit means every product shares the risk of every edit.

A test matrix nobody can write down

Whether one BPMN file is correct depends on products, times config rows, times flags. The 64 workflow-config migrations include repeated pairs that correct each other — pd-model-df4w-set-active, then underwriting-return-set-inactive-except-df4w. That is what a hidden matrix looks like in practice: about 2 corrective migrations a month since March 2024.

Things go quietly wrong instead of loudly failing

Because BaseActivity returns quietly, a missing config row does not fail a deployment or a process. It just removes a check from a loan product — and history still shows the step completing. There is no alert for this kind of defect today.

Nothing you can hand a product owner

You cannot show a stakeholder a diagram of their product. The diagram is shared, and the differences live in SQL and Java. Who owns which product is not recorded anywhere in the model — candidateGroups is empty on every user task, and assignment happens in code.

The duplication keeps growing

The older generation holds 156 different delegate beans, and the two big monoliths share only 20 of them. Most of the rest is the same domain step under a slightly different name. Duplicating everything per product is how NDF4W and NDF2W drifted apart, and it keeps happening wherever a product needs one step done differently.

What is not a reason to do this

Engine cost. Bravo’s workflow tier is the cheapest of the three platform options, at about Rp58M a month. This programme is about being able to read the system, limiting what one change can break, and correctness. It is not about savings, and it will not pay for itself in infrastructure.

One hard prerequisite

Securing the engine is not optional, and it cannot be left until later. /camunda is set to permitAll, and /engine-rest gives full control of the engine to anyone holding the shared key. That is how 61 hostile process definitions reached production. Nothing else on this plan should start while an outsider can deploy process definitions into the engine we are restructuring.

Explicitly out of scope

Rewriting survey or underwriting domain logic; migrating Sharia’s separate deployment beyond the spine work costed in Phase 4; and any change to the LORA or Temporal track. This plan is only about the shape of Bravo’s workflows, and about bringing product differences out into the open.
