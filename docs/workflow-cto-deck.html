The workflow is not the problem.
Where it hides product logic is.

An architecture review of Bravo's loan-origination workflows: what actually runs in production, the real gap against best practice, and a 30/60/90-day plan.

01

Bottom line up front

Recommendation

Give each product its own thin, readable workflow "spine" that calls shared domain steps. Make product differences visible in the model, not buried in config and code. Migrate the legacy monoliths onto it, highest-volume first.

The reframe

The premise was "one giant workflow serves every product." In production that is false. The shared workflow runs one product at 5.5% of volume. The giants are the legacy per-product monoliths carrying 94%.

The decision for you

Not "unwind a giant shared workflow." It is: the shared design has proven safe on DF4W. Do we migrate the legacy book onto it — with product-owned spines and explicit variation rather than today's hidden config?

04

The real gap: product logic is invisible

On the shared spine, what a product actually does is assembled from four places at once. None of them is the diagram.

Database config

A row can silently switch a step to a no-op. 55 steps are gated this way.

Java branches

if (application.isDF4W()) inside 19 of 68 shared steps; isDF4W() alone appears 20×.

BPMN gateways

3 product conditions embedded in the process XML.

Feature flags

9 flags in app config decide half-on / half-off per environment.

Measured, not hypothetical. On the shared spine, checks that don't apply to a product still appear in history as completed tasks — finishing in ~43 ms (a no-op) versus 900–1,900 ms for a real credit-bureau call. The diagram shows steps that never ran.
05

Why this costs you

Blast radius

One shared graph. A DF4W change redeploys the process every product runs. Changes to shared survey logic have touched the flow NDF2W depends on.

No owner-readable artifact

A product owner cannot be handed a diagram of their product. The differences live in SQL and Java, not a picture.

Hidden test matrix

Correctness of one file depends on products × config rows × flags. Repeated "set-active / set-inactive" fix-up migrations are that matrix leaking.

Silent divergence

A missing config row doesn't fail a deploy. It quietly removes a credit check from a loan product. No alarm.

Worth keeping Human work is already isolated in leaf steps; domain steps largely exist once; escalation and cancellation are centralised. The foundations are right.

08

The plan: 30 / 60 / 90 days

0–30dSee it & stop the bleeding
  • Publish an auto-generated flow diagram per product from the config
  • CI guard: block any new hidden product branch or no-op gate
  • Harden the workflow engine console (security prerequisite)
  • Sign off the target shape; delete dead config
Exit: every live product has an owner-readable flow
30–60dProve it on DF4W
  • Build an explicit spine · DF4W (already the only live spine product = lowest risk)
  • Remove its product gateways and hidden branches
  • Turn config no-ops into real, visible model steps
  • Per-product diagram diffed on every build
Exit: DF4W runs a thin owned spine, zero phantom tasks
60–90dMigrate the big one
  • Stand up spine · NDF2W (≈250k loans/quarter) on the shared children
  • Strangler-route rising % of new NDF2W volume
  • De-duplicate the worst 2W/4W step pairs
  • Write the decommission plan for NDF4W / RO / Sharia
Exit: ≥25% of new NDF2W on the spine at parity
09

Guardrails, scope, and one prerequisite

Prerequisite — do first

Harden the Camunda console. The workflow engine's admin surface was open to the network; 61 malicious process definitions were injected into production and one executed. This is written up separately. It must be closed before, or alongside, day-one work — the migration adds deployment activity to the same surface.

Why this sequence is low-risk

We prove the pattern on DF4W — the product already running the shared spine — before touching the high-volume legacy book. Nothing is big-bang; legacy stays live for rollback until its replacement carries real traffic cleanly.

Out of scope for 90 days

Rewriting survey/underwriting business logic; the separate Sharia deployment; and the LORA/Temporal track. This programme changes only the shape of the Bravo workflows — moving product logic out of hiding onto owned, readable spines.
